POWER SUPPLY SWITCHING STEP-UP

DC-DC in: 9-14V out: 12-18V max 10A "DIY" by R.Chirio

February 2007

This DC-DC power supply is a 1989 design of mine, still relevant today, and serves to raise 12V battery voltage and stabilize the output compensating for battery voltage drop during discharge. Capable of reaching 15V–16V it is indicated for obtaining maximum performance from radio transmission equipment. Also, with output voltage at 18V it can power Notebooks from car battery voltage.

Filters are present on the input and output to avoid interference from and to the radio equipment.

 The input voltage must be between 9.0 and 14.9V and the output voltage is adjustable from 14.0-18.0V When the input is 14.0V the maximum output current can reach 10A (5-10min without fan). The output voltage is adjustable via potentiometer from a minimum value of 12-14 V to a maximum of 18V.

There is no load limiting on the output (pay attention), there is only control of the maximum current through the MosFet. The 16A type T fuse protects the output from overload and short circuit.

 The best performance is obtained with 12-14V. Below 11.00Volt a battery-low alert occurs, to avoid completely discharging the battery.

UC1843

This regulator was chosen because it starts operating at 8.4V and stops at 7.6V. Perfect therefore for use with 12V batteries.

 The operating frequency is determined by:

f=1.72/(R8 C6)

This DC-DC power supply as you can see in the photo can reach 7A output in continuous service and 10A for a few minutes. If you plan to use it with maximum currents a small fan is necessary to cool the power section made up of Mosfet, Schottky diode, inductor and electrolytic capacitor.

Simplification:

For those who are not interested in the battery-low alert, just remove the LM358 circuit, the 4 inductors/capacitors on the input and output are also removable, in case radio equipment is not used.

Realization

The realization of the Switching Power Supply involves having knowledge of electronics, equipment and experience in electronic assembly are necessary.

No liability is accepted for damage to things and people and for improper use of the implementation.

Here is how the prototype appears mounted inside the metal box, it is possible to make a printed circuit board to better arrange the components and facilitate assembly, you can see the large electrolytic capacitor glued to the bottom. The filter coils are directly soldered to the input and output connectors.

The MosFet and Schottky Diode are fixed with a 3mm screw to the heatsink, for both it is necessary to put the insulating sheet underneath on the metal side to avoid electrical conduction on the aluminum heatsink.

The fuse holder is fixed with a 3mm screw on the rear panel, or more easily use a flying fuse holder, automotive type to solder on the battery power cable; power cables must be at least 4mm² in red and black color. Maximum length of 1.5m.

Inductor L5

The winding is made of 18 turns of enameled copper wire for a total of 4 wires diameter 0,75 on core L6A-55925-A. (MAGNETICS)

Arrange the winding around the entire circumference of the toroid, strip and solder together the start and end winding terminals.

The inductance has a value of 60 µH, during operation at current over 6 A it tends to heat up quite a bit, if not cooled it's good to make the winding with 7 copper wires diameter 0.75.

The inductor is soldered firmly on one end to the fuse holder terminal and on the other to the Drain of the Mosfet. To fix it to the bottom of the box, silicone can be used. It's good to keep it away from metal parts to avoid losses from stray induced currents.

Inductor L1-L4

These coils serve to block switching noise from the switching towards the power cables. Such interference can be heard in certain radio transceivers and on certain frequencies.

They are made with 10 turns of copper diam. 1.5-1.8 on ferrite with diameter 8-10mm, the winding is blocked with epoxy resin, it is possible instead to use the blocking coils present at the output of ATX power supplies.

The coils and related capacitors are also effective in blocking the radiofrequency that could interfere with the 1843 regulator.

Calibration

 Connect a variable power supply to the input and raise the voltage up to 12V, we should have the circuit working, adjust P2 to vary the output voltage. 

Connect a load of at least 3A and test the output voltage swing that must reach up to 18V.

Trimmer P1 is used to set the low battery warning threshold, reduce voltage down to 11.0 Volt and turn the trimmer to make the LED and buzzer light up (if used).

Reduced version 5A 18V out

March 2009

With this reduced version I wanted to satisfy the requests of those who have the need to power the notebook from the car battery at 12V. We have 5A output and a 18V voltage suitable therefore to power most laptop computers. With the trimmer, the voltage is however adjustable from 16 to 35V.

Furthermore the RF filters and the low battery indicator circuit have been removed from the circuit.

The circuit is simple, the size of electrolytic capacitors has been reduced, as well as the inductor. For dissipation as well, a small aluminum heatsink is sufficient.
