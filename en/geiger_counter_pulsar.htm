GEIGER COUNTER Calibrator

Pulse generator "Electronics design" by R.Chirio

  • To verify the operation of a Geiger counter you must also be sure that the count of the pulses generated by the GM tube is correct and consistent.
  • With this simple generator it is possible to send the entire range of pulses, such as to simulate most GM probes, and many scintillator tubes up to 600000cpm.
  • Those who already have a TTL pulse generator from the laboratory, can make only the simple final circuit composed of the MosFet and load resistor.
  • The accuracy of this calibrator must be read on a frequency counter, even an inexpensive one, connected to the oscillator output, so as to read values in Hz and convert them to CPM

electrical circuit diagram

Description

  • The circuit operates at 5V taken for convenience from a USB socket, consumption is only 10mA. However, it can work fine at 6V or even with a 9V battery. The voltage variation does not disturb the stability of the generated frequency.
  • Deliberately the components are easy to find, without using complicated quartz oscillators. Only two 555 integrated circuits were used.
  • The first U1 is in astable configuration, generates frequencies from 0,2 to 10 kHz necessary to simulate CPM (count for minute)
  • The frequency variation is adjusted with potentiometer R2 while for the range change the 4-position switch S1 is used.
  • The range of scales was chosen to better verify Geiger Counters with an index instrument, so that by turning potentiometer R2 you can make the needle move across the entire scale.
  • For verification of digital counters, all 4 ranges must be switched and the coherence of the readings verified.
  • On the Freq. Counter output you will need to connect a frequency counter or a digital oscilloscope and read the values with good precision, it's better to use a frequency counter, an inexpensive model for low frequency is enough.
  • The second 555 called U2 is used to generate the pulse that simulates the discharge duration that occurs inside a GM tube, the time is adjustable between 5 and 500uS. Check in the Counter manual what range of pulses it accepts on the input.

- The output represented by the Mosfet serves to simulate the GM Tube, short-circuiting the HV to ground via the 4M7 resistor. A 1000V Mosfet is necessary to test Geiger counters with output voltages from 400 to 900V; if you want to go up to 1300V for scintillation tubes a 1400V Mosfet is necessary which is harder to find, the rated current is very low so 1A Mosfets are sufficient. If Mosfet is not available it is possible to use transistors from the MJE13000 series

- The Stand-By switch allows isolating the output, perhaps to find the right values before operating the Geiger counter under test.

Calibration

  • Connect a high impedance voltmeter (300-1000Mohm) to the output and supply 5V.
  • The circuit doesn't require special tuning, it should work immediately, the generated square wave is visible by connecting the 10x scope probe to the output of U1 pin 3.
  • Turn potentiometer R2 and verify the frequency variation, as well as change the position of S1 for changing frequency bands.
  • Bring the oscilloscope probe to pin 3 of U2 and verify the width of the generated pulse.
  • Connect the geiger under test via insulated cables or with RG58 cable and BNC connectors.
  • Check the geiger output voltage with the high impedance voltmeter, bring to 400V
  • the closure of S5 will start the count on the geiger, verify consistency between generated and read.
  • Possible 900V test

Samples for Geiger calibration

Pitchblende samples under lead available for Geiger Counter calibration.

Containers diam. 29mm, values on request from 0.100 mRh to 2 mRh, emission type: 

  • Alpha, Beta + gamma (Free mineral)
  • Beta + Gamma (Mineral embedded in resin)
  • Only Gamma (Mineral shielded with aluminum 1mm)
