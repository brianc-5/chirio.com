Ni-Mh FAST BATTERY CHARGER

Automatic battery charger for 1-10 cells "DIY" by R.Chirio

Batteries & energyRoberto Chirio

April 2009

From the many requests for an effective battery charger for Ni-Mh, I developed this project that satisfies all users of Ni-Mh rechargeable batteries.

The circuit allows charging of all types of Ni-Mh batteries, the charging current is adjustable from 0 to 3 (5)A via a potentiometer.

The important characteristic is the disconnect at end of charge, which occurs when the batteries begin to heat, when that is they are charged. The relay disconnects the main power and the batteries remain isolated via the diode that prevents discharge.

To improve the efficiency of the constant current generator a switching configuration was used that works at 260 kHz, with efficiencies over 90%.

  

  • Sanyo Ni-Mh battery 2100mAh 1.2V type HR-4/5FAU

ELECTRICAL CIRCUIT DIAGRAM

Circuit description

Power supply with 220V 50 Hz input via transformer T1. The transformer secondary must be sized based on the maximum current you want to obtain; for a maximum of 3A a 50-60VA supply with 16-18V output is sufficient; to have 5A output with continuous service we need power of at least 75-100VA. A D2 diode bridge 4400mA 80V or greater rectifies the AC current and the C1 capacitor 4700uF has the task of filtering and smoothing. - The 220V section can be replaced with a notebook power supply 19-21V 3/5A connecting the output to the terminals of C1.

 The S1 button is used to provide power. If battery temperature conditions are below 30°C the relay closes and provides power to the charging circuit; if the battery is hot the system shuts down or is not powered. Clearly the R12 thermistor should be positioned under the charging batteries or better inside the battery pack and connected to the charging connector. Alternatively, use a Velcro strap to secure the thermistor on the surface of the battery pack being charged.

 The thermal protection circuit draws power from the output of the rectifier bridge and through the LM7812 regulator we obtain the 12V voltage needed to control the relay. During operation, RLY1 is normally closed. Trimmer R2 should be calibrated to open the relay at temperatures above 30-40° measured on the battery body. 

The U2 LM317 IC provides constant current for the current-regulation potentiometer, across the potentiometer there must be a voltage of 1.25-1.30V

 The U3 LM2678T-ADJ IC must be mounted on a heatsink; normally it works at constant voltage, but with this configuration we get very precise output current regulation instead. Note that the no-load voltage corresponds to that of C1, equal to 26-30V.

 Resistor R4 determines the maximum output current, its value is given by 1.25/Imax in the case of 5A we will have a value of 0.25 Ohm which is not a standard value, but is easily obtainable with the parallel of 4 resistors of 1ohm 2W.

 Diode D5 must be Schottky type, for 3A an MBR340 is fine, while for 5A use an MBR745 or similar.

 Coil L1 was made on green/blue cores diam. 12.70mm winding 25 turns of wire diam. 0.55mm

 With the potentiometer R3 we can adjust the current from a minimum of 10mA to a maximum of 5A, the adjustment is very precise and stable. It is necessary to have an ammeter at the output to precisely position the current value we need. I suggest mounting a knob with an index and writing the reference values.

 When batteries are charged, they begin to heat, therefore the thermal sensor (R12) must be positioned under the battery pack. If the temperature exceeds the calibration threshold, the battery charger shuts off automatically. The normal D4 diode rated at 10A prevents battery voltage from returning into the LM2678, damaging it.

 The NTC resistor R12 must be soldered on a small cable and positioned directly on the body of the accumulators under charge.

Conclusions

  • The system is very effective and useful for fast charging of any type of Ni-Mh batteries.
  • The switching configuration heats little and allows obtaining a powerful regulator with small dimensions.
  • The battery charger must be started with the accumulators already connected, otherwise there is risk of damaging the small accumulators from the C3 discharge which at no-load takes even 30V generating a strong DC discharge.
  • It is strongly recommended not to exceed the maximum currents recommended by the manufacturer, as batteries could even explode. Important to verify the thermistor function, which represents the automatic battery cutoff in case we are not present.
