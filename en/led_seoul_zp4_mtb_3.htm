BIKE-TRILED lamp

3 LED SEOUL ZP4 + FRAEN LENS FSP Economic solution to have a lot of light. over 2 hours of runtime with 12V 2600mA battery pack "DIY" by R.Chirio

September 2008.

Many ask me for a solution to have lots of light without spending much, this solution with three Seoul LEDs can give excellent results on the road.

We use a heatsink recovered from a CPU, three ZP4 LEDs and three Fraen FSP lenses.  

Current regulation is obtained with a single resistor in series with the 12V battery.

The power on the LEDs is about 10W, therefore we are close to 6-700 Lumen equal to a 50W halogen.

Battery you can use a series of 10 AA cells from 2600mA/h or also lead-acid batteries with higher amperage like the 12V 7Ah commonly used for alarm systems.

Seoul Z-LED P4 white star

power consumption max.: 3.7W (1000 mA)

luminous flux (max.): 240 lm (1000 mA)

color temperature (typ.): 6300K

Optical group.

 Optical unit composed of three FSP lenses with different exit angle, to better distribute the light.

In this case two Narrow lenses (10°) and one Medium lens (25°) will give us plenty of forward light with good side illumination.

 Whoever wants more light on the sides can mount two Medium Beam lenses and only one Narrow in the center.

 Finally, if you want all the light forward you can put three Narrow lenses.

  

Depending on the heatsink used, the mounting brackets can be adapted. In this case I used a 5mm stainless steel threaded rod 

The 68 x 30mm heatsink is sufficient to cool the 3 LEDs on when the vehicle is not moving, pedaling at 15Km/h the temperature drops below 30°.

The ZP4 LEDs are fixed to the heatsink simply by gluing them with CPU glue; transparent silicone can also be used with good results; it's important to use very little glue and press the LED very evenly toward the heatsink to minimize the thickness of the adhesive.

The FSP lenses are also fixed above the led with some silicone. Important that the lens is well seated and centered on the led, especially the Narrow lenses.

To improve the support at the solder joint and cable passage on the LED, with the soldering iron a little space is created by melting the black plastic of the lens.

Circuit diagram.

Mount the LEDs in series, using small-section wire, so as not to interfere with the base of the lenses.

The 3W resistors can be glued under the heatsink.

With switch S2 we can vary the current applied to the LEDs. With S2 open we are at approximately 350mA, when closed we are at approximately 850mA.

The power lost in the resistor is only 10% of the power consumed, conversion efficiency is good, unfortunately the current is not regulated, however using good quality batteries, the light fade during discharge is barely noticeable.

Flow/duration diagram

Test performed in laboratory, with amb. temp.=20°, LED unit cooled with fan. (Simulating about 15kmh.)

From the diagram we can read a runtime of 3.5 hours obtained with the 3.9ohm resistor.  

The measurement stops when the battery voltage reaches 10.5V that is when the battery is considered discharged, the flow is still 50% of the initial one.

The flux drop in the first 30 minutes is due to LED heating and battery voltage settling. The middle section is due to normal battery discharge.

With the use of the switching regulator we will have kept the flux constant at 90% until battery full charge.

Final remarks.

  • Easy implementation with good optical performance and easy adaptation, simply using lenses with different angle.
  • The configuration with three LEDs in series gives us good conversion efficiency of 90%.
  • With 2600mA battery pack we get approximately 1.8 hours of runtime at full power and 3.5 hours with the lowest resistance of 3.9 ohm.
  • With the S2 switch we can use maximum power only when necessary, therefore we achieve good battery saving to the advantage of runtime.
  • With the heatsink in question the operating temperature is approximately 10° higher than ambient, this with movement, when the bike is stationary I recommend turning it off because temperatures reach 50°.
