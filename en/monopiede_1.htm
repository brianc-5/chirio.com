cheap monopods - economical monopod

photography tips by R.Chirio

Photo & projectsRoberto Chirio

July 2005

Photonatural images, especially those of wild animals, are to be made exclusively with telephoto lenses and to achieve the best results it is mandatory to use heavy tripods (even with stabilized optics); to reach the locations of wildlife photography you must sometimes walk several hours, and unless you have a porter with you, the tripod should be left at home because of its weight; therefore a good compromise is the use of the monopod.

Considering that a monopod, branded, can even exceed 100 euros, I decided to build one spending very little, and also obtaining a good weight reduction compared to commercial ones.

I used ordinary trekking poles, light and no less robust than a camera monopod. Ordinary poles, with the stems open and well tightened, easily hold a load of 15kg. It's important to find telescopic poles that when fully extended reach at least 1.40 - 1.50 m.

The poles can continue to be used to reach the photo hunt location, after applying the threaded bushing their functionality is not reduced.

The realization requires a certain skill and equipment for DIY. The final cost is close to zero euros, if at home we already have the sticks, the glue and the threaded rod.

material:

● 1 or two economical telescopic trekking poles, type Quechua Forclaz 300 available at Decathlon

● 1 brass threaded rod minimum length 55mm, 3/8 inch thread gas pitch (*)

● 1 brass washer external diameter 30mm internal 10mm thickness 5mm (*)

● two-component acrylic adhesive type Bostik Plastica Mixer (very good!)

(*) It is not easy to find, it must be ordered at the hardware store, if you have a friend who can machine a single piece that is even better.

realization

The camera mount was made for only one of the sticks and with a 3/8 inch thread, the large one not suitable for camera attachment, but for tripod heads and large telephoto lenses. Optionally, to attach the camera just screw a 1/4 connector or a small ball head from a tripod.

Cut the threaded rod to size, or turn the complete flanged piece 5 mm thick and 30mm diameter. The thread on the right part is not necessary, as it stays inside the handle of the stick and will be fixed with acrylic glue.

Drill the rear part of the stick with a 3mm bit, first extend the center part of the stick, to avoid damaging the internal part when the bit enters the tube. Enter with the bit for a maximum of 60mm, continue to ream the hole with a 6mm bit and then an 8mm bit, finally a 10mm bit, pass the bit multiple times to have a burr-free path.

Try to insert the prepared threaded rod of the right size, if it goes in easily all the way through and the washer rests well on the rubber of the stick knob, we can now proceed to glue everything together. Warning: the suggested glue has strong grip and hardens in a few minutes. Before mixing the glue, make sure everything is in place for assembly.

The amount of glue required is about a teaspoon. Mix the two parts well in equal percentages. Put about half the adhesive in the hole, the remaining part on the thread and under the washer.

Push firmly and with force the threaded rod assembly, the upper part where we will screw the head or tele must not protrude more than 6.5mm to not damage the attachments that will be applied.

After about 15 minutes the glue will be hardened.

All that's left is to test our monopod, secure a head on the thread and tighten slightly, remember that the glue may not be completely cured. If in doubt about the glue wait 24 hours. 

Verify the solidity of the assembly and then apply a camera or telephoto lens and have fun with your new monopod.

Optionally the second rod can be equipped with a 1/4 inch thread, so you can mount directly a camera or small telephoto lens.

The excellent monopiede head, Manfrottto 234 can be advantageously used on normal tripods.

This solution allows us to have a very light head, still lowered and stable, more so than a normal three-movement head.

I use this solution for when you have to walk in the mountains and over 500g less weight is always handy.....
