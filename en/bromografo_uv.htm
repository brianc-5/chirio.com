UV bromograph for printed circuits

Made using a disused scanner. "DIY" by R.Chirio

May 2007.

All those who deal with electronics as a hobby or profession must also make printed circuit boards for mounting the electronic components of various projects.

When the need is for a few pieces for experimentation, it is very economical to fabricate the printed circuit boards yourself.

This excellent homemade bromograph is built inside a retired scanner. This one in particular is a TAMARAK, a very robust scanner with the entire body in aluminum extrusion and the bottom in sheet metal. The internal height is 4cm, good for fitting UV neon tubes and their ballasts inside.

A timer with a fixed time of 2 minutes is sufficient for good etching on presensitized boards. To extend the application to various types of boards, it can be useful to adjust the time from 30" to 5 minutes.

Electronic timer

Using a specific Siemens IC it is possible to create a programmable timer, operating directly at 220V.

SAB 0529 timer (download Data Sheet pdf - 516kb) SAE 0530 also works well

Diagram on page 500 Data Sheet, instead of the motor connect the neon reactor unit. Program for 2 minutes.

REALIZATION

Once the most suitable scanner for this use is identified, it must be emptied of all mechanisms and electronic boards. Only the body with the glass and the cover must remain. Optionally the connector and the switch can be kept if it's 220V.

Fix the 8 neon supports on the bottom and position them at equal distances to evenly distribute the UV light over the entire glass surface.

Attach the two ballasts and 4 starters to the sides or front, they can be placed in any position, important that they don't interfere with the light emitted by the tubes.

Line the entire bottom, under the tubes with adhesive reflective aluminum, to have good reflection upwards.

Connect the tubes with their respective starters and with the series of ballasts.

Connect the main switch with the timer and verify the power-on time using a stopwatch.

You can also use a mechanical timer recovered from a microwave oven.

If all is well you can proceed with testing, all tubes should light up and have the same intensity.

Proceed with the sensitization of a test board according to the supplier's data.

It has been verified that 2 minutes is the right time for good etchings on presensitized boards normally in commerce.

......

IMPROVEMENT

For the more experienced, you can remove the reactor and starter, and use electronic ballasts, recovered from exhausted low-consumption neon lamps.

In this case, using the electronic group of single-tube lamps of 7-10W or those with double tube of 16-20W, we can power the 8W UV tubes with the advantage of immediate ignition and increased luminous efficiency.

Moreover not least, we help recover at zero cost, a working electronic board that would end up in landfill for nothing.

...2013. For the more experienced, you could consider using UV LED diodes instead of tubes, but high-power UV diodes are quite expensive; therefore it is advisable to use 5mm LED diodes. The LED system offers advantages because it can be powered at 12V and built on recent low-profile scanners.

Building the bromograph requires skills in electrical assembly, minimum equipment is necessary, such as a solder iron, solder, electrical cables, various electronic tools, drill with bits, etc.
