Power a 20mA LED with 1.5Volt AA Cell

This is the simplest circuit to properly power a 20mA white LED from a 1.5Volt battery like an AA cell or just an AAA cell.

This is a one-transistor oscillator in self-oscillating configuration with a Step-up recovery stage at the output.

The transistor Q1 goes into self-oscillation as T1 feeds the collector signal to the base with 180° phase.

The working frequency and consequently the current in the LED is determined by the value of R1, by the inductive values of T1 and the characteristics of Q1. The load represented by D1, C2 and D2 LED also contributes to the oscillation frequency.

In this case I used a generic transistor, suitable for switching:

 type UTC2SA684 PNP VCE:60V Ic:1A f:200 MHz hfe:85

In general pretty much any transistor in this range works, better if NPN (more common), in this case, remember to reverse the polarity of the battery, capacitors and diodes.

Transformer T1 about 20 turns of enameled copper wire diam. 0.15-0.2mm wound bifilar on a 10mm toroid, also works on a small ferrite cylinder (6x8), or for good performance, a small E-core ferrite transformer would be ideal.

0.487

Waveform at Q1 CE terminals

For visual clarity I have inverted top/bottom in the figure, the voltage has a negative trend upward, since we use a PNP transistor.

With an NPN we will see in normal the same waveform.

Comments and improvements:  

In this case using a distributed air-gap toroid as the core, it's not possible to have very good efficiency, I would suggest trying with a dual E transformer core in N27 ferrite.

Further improvements using a Schottky diode instead of D1.

This configuration is not suitable for providing higher power as higher currents and conduction times tend to waste a lot of energy on the core and transistor, bringing the system to very low efficiency.

You can try to squeeze performance to power up to 4 LEDs in parallel or a single 80mA 10mm diameter LED. (see below)

The search and selection of transistors with better characteristics can give significant improvements.

The circuit also works with a 1.2V rechargeable battery it is necessary to modify the value of R1 to have 20mA on the LED.

If you use for power a good quality 1.5 torch (D cell) we can have over 100 hours of runtime, which considering the good output of the LED at 40000mcd (improvable with new ones at 50000mcd) we get a compact and reliable light source. 

February 2007

Improved version, using a 6x8 cylindrical ferrite transformer improves the transistor output and therefore it is possible to have a higher current at the output, in this case I put 18+18 turns 0.15 diameter.

R1 becomes 270 ohm and at the output it's possible to drive a 10mm LED at 80mA 120000mcd, the efficiency of this 4 times more powerful configuration remains about 0.48, to use 1.2V rechargeable batteries adapt the R1 value to get 80mA current in the LED.

Power an LED 20mA with 1.5Volt AA Cell improved version with current stabilized at 20mA/led

Further improved version using a second transistor to block the oscillator when the LED current exceeds 20mA. It's sufficient to monitor the current in only one branch to also stabilize the current on the other diodes; it's important that all LED diodes are of the same type.

The value of R1 must be adjusted to have operation up to about 1.0V. The exact value depends on the components used and the type of transformer.

Using the latest high-efficiency led, and a torch battery (type D) we can make a reliable safety lamp with long runtime.
