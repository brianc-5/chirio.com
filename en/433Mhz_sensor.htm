Sensors at 433 MHz

Premise

September 2013/January 2020

Anyone wanting to use a digital thermometer or weather station with an external wireless probe often cannot reach outside the house due to lack of signal, whereas in open space they reach even 30m; the problem is the frequency and the poor antenna mounted on the sensors. Another problem is radio signal saturation; in the presence of strong fields from WiFi or commercial FM radio, small signals at 433 MHz are penalized.

Another problem is that most sensors almost always use the same 433 module and 3 Ch in a condominium context, or nearby houses, often it's not enough anymore, you might end up reading the temperature from your neighbor's probe...

Solution, just improve the small antenna to increase range.

Sensor Oregon Scientific

Removing the back cover reveals the spiral coil which functions as an antenna; unfortunately the output signal is partly absorbed by the metal batteries and the NTC sensor positioned right under the coil. (These connections seen as signal grounding.) Also it is not a good solution to have the antenna coil with the wire running together with the sensor; almost all the RF gets cancelled.

With 433 MHz the wavelength is 70cm, theoretically any obstacle closer to the transmitter can penalize transmission efficiency.

Likewise the passage of the enamel-coated copper antenna wire near the CS penalizes the signal, a shielded coaxial cable should be used.

A better solution would be to leave the NTC in place and exit with the antenna in the upper part of the box, it would need to be well sealed to prevent water entry.

Modification with antenna output from the sensor side.

  

Description

To simplify and avoid waterproofing problems, the antenna wire was chosen to exit from the bottom.

The modification is very simple; gently detach the coil and unwind all the enameled copper wire. The wire length is excessive to resonate at 1/4 wavelength at 433 MHz; we need to shorten it to 17cm. In this case, a Teflon tube diam. 0.8 was used for protection and anti-abrasion.

The cable is brought out from one of the holes for measuring air temperature, the NTC sensor must be positioned in the center and fixed with some thermal glue.

The result is already very good, the range outside the house has multiplied by 3 and even WiFi presence doesn't interfere much. We can easily cover 3 floors where before barely one floor was covered with the internal antenna.

Beware that some sensors use the frequency of 315 MHz so the length of the 1/4 antenna must be 23cm. the operating frequency is written on the back of the device or better on the SAW Resonator.

This modification works well for all types of remote sensors, even those for Power Meter use the same frequency and RF group with the 433 MHz SAW Resonator.

This is the generic circuit diagram of the RF section where L2 represents the internal antenna coil which we eliminated by using the 17cm wire.

For those wanting to go further, the best solution would be to match the output impedance for 52 Ohm and solder an RG 174 cable connected to an SMA connector to then go to a proper antenna, positioned at the best point. It's conceivable to reach 80-100 meters.....

Adding the 17cm antenna also on the receiver can further improve performance, the receiver should be positioned not hanging on the wall, but on a non-metallic table far from obstructions, so as to have better reception opening.

Even better to add a small RF amplifier in class "A" to bring the signal from 5-10mW to safer 100-200mW......there would be no great battery consumption at 9V since the data transmission pulse lasts less than 1 sec every 30 sec....

Simple solution

For those who don't want to open and solder, I propose this solution, less effective but that can give some results: a dipole antenna coupled directly to the sensor, consisting of winding two turns of stiff wire and leaving the ends as antenna; in the case of 433 MHz the whips should be left 17 cm long, the winding should be positioned where the internal antenna is underneath. Moving the transmitter away you should notice signal stability.
