MTB STUDDED TIRES

How to make a pair of ice tires. "DIY" by R.Chirio

Photo & projectsRoberto Chirio

January 2009

What are studded tires (ice tires) used for on an MTB bicycle?

To many purists this is not of interest, but those who want to have fun on MTB even in winter, two tires with studs, extend the possibility of having fun.

I made the tires a few years ago, then they ended up in the basement, since in recent years snow and ice were scarce, surely this winter, given the heavy snow and low temperatures, even in the plains, they're back in fashion!

Going on a frozen lake, or even just on frozen and compact snow, it's incredible, the grip, traction and ability to lean in curves that these studded tires offer. 

To avoid falling, you must also have shoes with nails, otherwise the first time you put your foot down, you slip....

material:

● 2 tires 26" with large lugs spaced apart. Size 2.10" or 2.20" (Budget tires also work fine)

● 2 tires 26" for road, even smooth. Size 1.80"

● 4-500 wood screws 4x15 or better self-tapping screws 4x16.

realization

Making the studded tire with Parker screws doesn't involve great difficulty, but you still need to pay attention to the various operations of drilling the tire and screwing in the screws.

In practice 100 studs per tire are few and do not ensure good grip, especially on the side. I recommend staying around 200 studs per tire, as a greater number would certainly give excellent grip but there's considerable weight and road friction is also significant.

- The Parker screws must be screwed in from the inside of the casing, it's a long job so you need to get a good electric screwdriver. To have a reference for where to insert the screws, it is necessary to drill from outside with a 2mm tip on each anchor point where you want to insert the pin (screw). The drilling must be done from outside to inside respecting the centering of the anchor and its opening angle. This operation must be done well, with precision, obviously with the casing not mounted on the wheel....

Wood screws 4x15

- These screws are of excellent steel, very hard. They are however too sharp and dangerous, once screwed in it is good to give a grinding to the tip so as not to hurt yourself.

Self-tapping screws 4x16

  • Excellent steel, very hard, the tip is hard and holds well, these screws can be considered a better solution than the previous ones, they are not so sharp as to be dangerous, so it is not necessary to grind the tips.
  • After completing the drilling operations, we can proceed to screw in about 200 tire screws. Using a power drill in 20 minutes you can prepare a tire.
  • The screws must be screwed gently all the way without forcing, otherwise the rubber cracks easily.
  • At this point we can mount everything on the rim. You need to insert the road tire inside the studded cover, now become spiked. The road tire serves to hold the screw heads in position, otherwise under load they would enter inside the studded cover destroying the inner tube in short order.

  • The road tire generally fits inside without removing the beading, in particular cases where the rim seat is particularly tight it's necessary to cut the beading and keep only the external tread part.
  • Instead of the inner tube covering you can mount a protective layer in Kevlar or Mylar, to prevent the screw heads from damaging the inner tube; it has been tested that the smooth road tire is the most robust solution that allows for many kilometers without problems with the screws, important is inflation to 3-4 Atm.
  • Finish the assembly with the proper tools to install the tire. Check that everything fits well in the rim seat and that the inner tube is not pinched.

The tires in the photo were studded 10 years ago so there are no photos showing how to screw the screws in, but it's not so hard to imagine, just use a good screwdriver.

  • The tire must be inflated to 3-4 ATM because under load there is much pressure on the screw heads, particularly when traveling off ice/snow, that is on rocks and asphalt.
  • Avoid as much as possible going over rocks and asphalt and be careful of any falls.......the nails (screws) leave their mark......
