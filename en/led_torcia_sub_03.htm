January, 2008

LED underwater torches represent a significant evolution compared to halogen lamp illuminators, light output increases by more than 4-5 times compared to the traditional quartz lamp.

The luminous spectrum of the LEDs towards white blue, facilitates the penetration of light into the water.

It is fairly easy to modify a TECHNISUB underwater torch model Nuova Vega, removing the reflector with the halogen lamp and mounting in its place an optical group consisting of 3 Seoul ZP4 LEDs, current regulator and heatsink plate.

Watch the video created by sub Ivan:

http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pQzjS8gDg1k

Optical group.

- The assembly consisting of LEDs with 10° lenses, the switching power supply, the charging connector. All mounted on an aluminum plate 125mm in diameter 10mm thick.

  • The unit is fixed by means of a 4 MA tie with a central hole on the aluminum plate.
  • For closing the large knob of the halogen lamp focus is used. The lamp socket assembly is removed and a 4MA threaded bushing remains where we will screw in the pull rod.

- The group of 3 LEDs is fixed with 3MA screws, in through-holes threaded directly into the aluminum plate. It is necessary to apply some conductive grease between the base of the LEDs and the heatsink plate. 

Circuit diagram for batteries from 7.2 to 12V

The original magnetic switch is kept as the steady-state current draw is less than 2A, lower than the old halogen lamp.

It is good to put a 6.3AT fuse in series with the accumulators, to protect against short circuits.

The Battery Charging jack allows us to charge the accumulators without removing them from the container.

The three LEDs at the output are connected in parallel, to better balance the currents it is necessary to have small section cables all of the same length.

Circuit diagram for batteries from 4.8 to 6V

The original magnetic switch is kept as the steady-state current draw is less than 3A, lower than the old halogen lamp.

It is good to put a 6.3AT fuse in series with the accumulators, to protect against short circuits.

The Battery Charging jack allows us to charge the accumulators without removing them from the container.

The three LEDs at the output are connected in parallel, to better balance the currents it is necessary to have the small section cables all of the same length, it is necessary to put in series with each LED a 1.2 Ohm 3W resistor for 4.8V batteries. Or with 6V it is necessary to put a 2.5 Ohm 3W resistor.

Realization

  • The Battery Charger socket is fastened with acrylic glue directly on the aluminum.
  • The switching regulator is fastened to the heatsink plate with one or two 3MA screws.
  • The cable passage must be protected with a piece of heat-shrink tubing, to avoid tearing of the cables themselves.
  • The lenses must be fixed with a drop of adhesive for each pin.

  • You can see inside the torch the 4MA pull rod controlled by the red knob.
  • The accumulator pack from 8000mA/h
  • Before closing everything check that the accumulators are well positioned and the contact tabs do not press on the body of the accumulators themselves. 
  • Close carefully without pinching the connection cables, with battery power it's easy to create a disastrous short circuit with cable burning.

  • Torch in charging phase
  • With the 8000mA/h battery pack, and 600mA battery charger we obtain a complete recharge in 10-14 hours.
  • Before closing the front glass remember to put silicone grease on the gasket.
  • Runtime was more than 4 continuous hours with the LEDs powered at 850mA each.

Torch 9 LED Seoul

  • Modification to underwater torch with original 9 Luxeon LEDs
  • Good luminous performance replacing the Luxeon with 9 Seoul ZP4.
  • Excellent dissipation directly in water, obtained through the aluminum torch body.
  • Modest runtime due to battery of only 6V 5A/h, it was not possible to mount the switching regulator which would have improved conversion efficiency.
