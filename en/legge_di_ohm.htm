Considerations

March 2007

Based on what I read on the web and also on the content of some emails I receive, it seems to me that the simple Ohm's law is not so clear to everyone.....

...Ohm's Law establishes that, in every conductor crossed by an electric current, a potential difference (or voltage drop) is produced between its ends, this depends on the resistance of the conductor itself (resistivity).

High resistances correspond to high voltage drops, while low resistances correspond to small voltage drops, all this with equal current flowing.....

Diagram of principle:

Given a DC voltage source B, and a load R connected in series. A current I will flow through the load. The product between Voltage and Current determines the power P on the load R.

The voltage (V) must be measured across the load, therefore in parallel, the current (I) passing through the load must be measured in series with the load.

If we measure current in parallel with the load, the ammeter is generally destroyed, if we measure voltage in series with the load, nothing happens.....the circuit opens because Voltmeters have high input resistance.

Relationship between Parameters V, I, R and P

Some examples:

Let's connect a 12 ohm resistor to a 12V battery, what is the power on the resistor?

from the Power relationship we obtain (V x V) / R from which (12 x12)/12 = 12Watt

Always with a 12V battery, what resistor should I put to have a current of 10A and what power must the resistor dissipate?

R=V/I 12V/10A=1.2 ohm the power is: V x I = 12 x 10= 120W
