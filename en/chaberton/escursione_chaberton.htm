The Chaberton Battery

Excursion

From June to the end of September, if weather conditions are good, it's possible to walk to the summit of Chaberton (3130m); there are four possible routes, three on foot only, a fourth, the longest, can also be done by mountain bike with difficulty. All starting points are easily reachable from SS 24, then park the vehicle and set out on foot for the excursion.

The routes are indicated for the majority of people, they are carried out almost entirely on military dirt roads and on mule tracks always traced during the war period. In routes (1) and (2) there are no alpine climbing difficulties, but it is always necessary to have good mountain shoes and pay attention to where you put your feet, especially if you enter inside the fortified works. It is necessary to have water, at least 1.5l per person, because in the summer period beyond Colle Chaberton it is not possible to find spring water. It is advisable to have mountain clothing and not underestimate the climb.

CLIMBING ROUTES TO MT. CHABERTON see Chaberton map

1) Climb from the old military road that starts from Fenils (the longest, over 13km) walkable without major issues since the slope is never steep. The lower part is largely passable by mountain bike but is defined by experienced cyclists as nearly impossible from Vallone dei Morti to the summit. (5/7 hours elevation gain 1700mt)

2) Route Claviere - Rio Secco Valley classic route (the easiest on foot), most people climb via this path, starting on foot from Claviere or parking the car after the French customs. (3/4 hours, elevation gain 1300m)

3) Route Petit Vallon - Ferrata  (the shortest but also the most difficult) basic mountaineering preparation is necessary to tackle the final section. The route is much more scenic compared to ascents n.1 and n.2 as it runs along the south ridge overlooking Cesana. (3/5 hours elevation gain 1450mt) Warning to take the path after the avalanche barrier the stairs no longer exist, now there is a construction site, it is not easy to find a passage.

4) An alternative to the first three routes may be the mule track of Cresta Nera, which starts directly from Cesana, behind the Carabinieri Barracks and climbs steeply until it intersects the former Fenils military road. (4/5 hours) Difficult and rarely hiked to the Cresta Nera Observatory, then continues on the military path of route 1. (4/5 hours elevation gain 1600mt)

All routes begin on Italian territory and then continue on French territory. French gendarmerie checks and controls are reported, avoid bringing down objects found inside and around the Chaberton battery.
