Premise

May 2014 

This circuit represents a radio frequency (RF) signal detector, it indicates to us the presence of radio emission over a wide spectrum of frequencies from a few thousand Hz to over 2000 MHz signaling with the LED turning on the 3G cellular lock signal.

Given the good input sensitivity it can signal the presence of unexpected radio emissions, like detecting spy radios (radio bugs), it's also useful for checking switching power supplies that emit strong interference annoying to those wanting to listen to shortwave.

It can also be used to check the amplified TV antenna signal level.

The proposed circuit does not perform a signal measurement, but only short-range detection.

On the BNC input it is possible to connect various antennas best suited to receive the frequency to analyze.

Operation is guaranteed by a 9V battery, standby consumption is less than 1 mA.

Circuit diagram

  

  • On the input you need to connect an antenna, even a few cm is enough to detect the signal, for greater sensitivity at lower frequencies a stylus of at least 40-50 cm is recommended.
  • Capacitor C1 cuts domestic frequencies below 10 kHz, by adjusting R1 we can change the input sensitivity, far from RF sources, we will adjust R1 to turn off the LEDs at the intervention threshold.
  • There are no inductors, so the input accepts a wide spectrum of frequencies. In place of R2 it's possible to put a tuned circuit made up of a variable capacitor and inductor, so as to have a selective circuit only for the frequency we're interested in.
  • The radio signal is detected by the D1 diode which for better sensitivity must be germanium, the circuit also works with a common Si diode like the 1N4148 or similar, you can also use the base-emitter junction of an RF transistor.

- The detected signal converted to a value of a few mV is applied to the C3 capacitor which smooths it to a time constant such as to avoid rapid LED flashing, the LM 358 in 0V comparator configuration brings the output to positive values as soon as the signal exceeds the offset threshold which for the LM358 is 3-4mV. This is the minimum signal value we will read at input. To improve sensitivity I recommend using more expensive operational amplifiers with offset threshold less than 1mV.

  • The next stage in follower configuration acts as a unity gain amplifier to directly drive the LEDs with a max current of 15mA, more than good to give strong brightness to the 3 red 5mm LEDs in series.
  • 3 RED LEDs were used to have strong light signaling with low losses on the resistor.
  • The RF indicator is clearly visible at several meters, even in daylight....

Realization

Here the circuit assembled inside a small metal box. The simplicity of the diagram allows for quick assembly using even just a breadboard.

The BNC connector allows us to apply the most varied types of antenna and also cables connected to specific antennas.

The battery in this case is connected directly without a switch since the low current draw gives us many tens of hours of operation.

The three LEDs are fixed inside with a bit of cyanoacrylic adhesive 

 Detail on the input stage, to detect frequencies beyond 1000 MHz it is good to use a small trimmer, as well as take care with component placement.

These are the minimum input signal values that will turn on the LEDs, the meter still reads cell phone signals well up to 2100 MHz as well as with attenuation WiFi signals at 2400 MHz, using an RF diode and a piece of antenna of only 3.5cm these signals are revealed better beyond 2000 MHz.

By setting capacitor C1 to 0.1uF it is possible to have LED on even with 50 Hz electromagnetic field.

To work with very low input signals, a FET amplifier capable of reading uV values can be added, however the circuit must be reviewed to reduce input noise.

Applications

The LED RF Meter is useful for displaying the presence of radio frequency even at low values, always harmful to health, as well as you can spot hidden transmitting spy microphones, or useful in the electronics lab to verify the operation of RF circuits.

Useful for adjusting the signal of TV systems, first adjust the trimmer to make the LED light up at an outlet where the signal is correct, then use the RF LED METER on the outlet to be measured and adjust the TV antenna amplifier to give the same signal.
