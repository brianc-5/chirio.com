Volt Meter 1000Mohm

Accurate HV meter for a GM Counter "Electronics design" by R.Chirio

RadioactivityRoberto Chirio

  • To verify the operation of a Geiger counter you must also be sure that the high voltage output is correct, and at the correct value for the probe in use.
  • Generally Russian probes work from 400 to 500V, American probes 900V and Scintillation probes 1200V.
  • The output voltage is almost static, that is, it is unable to supply power, therefore a Voltmeter with the highest possible internal impedance is needed so as not to interfere by loading the output and thus lowering the actual reading. 
  • It has been found that with 300 MΩ a correct reading is already possible, but as recommended by some American manufacturers, 1000 MΩ is the optimal value for a precise output reading.

Very high input impedance voltmeter. 1000:1 ratio max 1999 Volt

With this circuit it is possible to measure the voltages applied to devices that operate at high voltage, such as Geiger tubes that range from 300 to 1500V. With normal digital multimeters and analog testers, it is not possible to measure such values because with the low input impedance (10Mohm) they tend to load the circuit under test too much, greatly falsifying the values read.

  • The most critical component is the 1000Mohm resistor which can be ordered online : code SM102031007F
  • Diodes and zener on the input circuit serve as protection for pulses and discharges that could damage the operational input. They can also be omitted.
  • R3 and R5 make up the lower part of the divider with a 990:1 ratio
  • An operational amplifier in voltage follower configuration allows impedance matching with high input impedance and low output impedance. Various types of operational amplifiers can be used, important with low offset value, or better adjustable offset like the uA741. The follower configuration has a gain of 1. With the LM358 we have an offset value of 3-5 mV.

- The R4 trimmer is used to precisely calibrate the 1000:1 ratio; for calibration use a 220V rectifier circuit as voltage reference and measure the exact value with a multimeter typically 310V DC (be careful) Connect a 1.999 Vfs voltmeter or multimeter set to the same scale at the circuit output, read the input value and calibrate R4 to have the same value as read before with just the multimeter.

  • The power consumption of the instrument is very low, a few mA so the two 9V batteries last for many hundreds of hours. 
  • The DC input voltage can have a range of +/-2000Volt. It is possible to measure AC voltages up to a frequency of 100Kz.
  • If you want greater precision I recommend using Operational Amplifiers with fine OFFSET adjustment, like the UA741 or other more recent ones.
  • If it is enough to read only positive HV voltages it is possible to eliminate the V2 battery and also on the D1 and D2 input, pin 4 must be brought to ground, simplifying everything.
  • You can see the two 9V batteries one for the circuit and the second only for the milliVoltmeter LCD.
  • The 1000Mohm resistor is made with a series of 2 500Mohm resistors
  • The resistors are isolated by two sheets of mylar, to prevent discharges towards ground and towards the batteries.
  • On the rear the switch and BNC connector, I recommend installing quality BNC with Teflon insulation since not all of them handle 2000V.

  

The HV Voltmeter connected to a Scaler, without HV reading.

300Mohm resistor made with 30 SMD 10Mohm resistors. The resistors are protected by a layer of Araldite. The construction is very simple and the resin insulation guarantees an operating voltage up to 5000Volt.

Simplified version of the HV voltmeter, to be connected directly to a Multimeter (DMM). On the 2V range, we will read x1000 therefore KVolts. The multi-turn cermet trimmer is used to precisely calibrate the 1000:1 ratio.

Important: the DDM must have 10Mohm input impedance, some low-cost ones have only 1Mohm.
