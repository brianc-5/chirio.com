Wire antenna loaded for 40mt Band

August 2015-January 2021

For once this is not my project but an antenna already proposed on the web by radio amateur GM4JMU see: http://www.qsl.net/g3pto/shortant.html

A half-wave dipole in my case would take up too many meters so difficult to arrange around or above the house, therefore this solution is within feasibility.

The realization is done with 2.5mmq electrical cable normal cable for electrical installations any color, I recommend black as it is less visible.

You need to make the two 40-turn coils, the insulators and the central Boom as well as the Balun.

The total length from insulator/insulator is 10.12 meters. For best performance the entire antenna must be positioned high and away from obstacles. I would recommend 4 meters as minimum height but 10 meters would be ideal, clearly away from obstacles.

The external arm of about 2.30m should be cut longer, 2.50m since during calibration it can be useful to have a greater length, there's always time to shorten. Also the operating frequency depends greatly on the accuracy and size of the coils.

Realization

  • The loading coil serves to reduce the length of the two dipole arms and must be built respecting the measurements and positioned at the exact point.
  • Use a 40 mm diameter hydraulic PVC tube, the length is 18-20 cm and also depends on the diameter of the electrical wire used, in my case the cable diameter is 3.9 mm. 
  • Wind 40 turns as in photo, just make a hole of 4,5mm at the start and end of tube to have fastening and wrapped cable outlet.
  • The length of the wrapped cable is about 5mt, the two arms plus the coil cable correspond to a half-wave dipole.
  • I advise making a single continuous cable run without soldering, it's all safer and simpler and can help to remove and add turns if the operating frequency is not centered. Prepare a single 12 meter cable run for each arm of the dipole.

  

  • This is the central boom made from a 4mm thick bakelite board, fiberglass or sturdy plastic material that resists water and sun (UV) works well (better).
  • Solder the two RG59/U cables to the head of two brass bolts, 5 or 6mm are fine closed with the nut.
  • In this case I decided not to make the Balun because I lack the suitable toroid core, I will nonetheless use the impedance adapter "75 OHM matching 1/4 wave stubs" made with 6.90m of RG59 cable (length calculated as 1/4 wavelength x 0.66).

 At the end of the 75 ohm cable the RG213/U 50 ohm cable is connected for the length needed to reach the transmitter.

Characteristics

  • After appropriate shortening we arrived at this situation, center band 7.150 MHz and SWR of 1.2 all measured at the end of the line (19m) before entering the radio. 
  • Considering that the automatic tuner of the Yaesu FT450D works up to an ROS (SWR) of 3 we have an acceptable bandwidth that goes from 7.0 MHz to 7.30 MHz

To go even lower in frequency you had to have longer cables...for that reason I recommend starting with 2.5m and then cutting until you reach the right value.

In the absence of an analyzer you will have to do some measurements with the Rosmetro to find the working point.

Improvements

 

  • For those who want to reduce weight and dimensions, it is possible to replace the large coil with a toroidal core that must have the same inductance value, certainly the "Q" changes which modifies the bandwidth.
  • The wire coil has a value of 48uH (7 MHz), using the AMIDON core table you find the most suitable core for the frequency range and then you wind the necessary turns to reach the value.
  • Another variant is to make the same antenna with 4mmq cable, it has a slightly wider bandwidth, but everything becomes heavier....
  • Many make the coil with enameled copper wire with a diameter of 0.8 - 1.2mm, the value reached must be measured, the 40 turns might not work. 

Dual band antenna loaded 7 MHz and 27.5 MHz

  • With slight variations in the branch lengths it is possible to have the charged dipole resonate also at 27.5 MHz or 28 MHz.
  • It is no longer a horizontal wire but must be positioned as an "inverted V" that is with an angle of 120° so as to bring the impedance to 50 Ohm, thus the stub made with 75Ohm cable is eliminated, the 50 Ohm is connected directly on the central boom better if with good quality cable and size like RG213 or even just RG8X
  • To center the frequencies perhaps the two inner arms from 2.58m should be lengthened by a few cm, as well as lengthen the outer arms from 2.30 by 5cm
  • This way we will have a wire antenna able to transmit with over 100W applied to both resonant bands.

Propagation

- In this last period of 2015 there are often poor propagation conditions, due to "Solar Spots" which could cancel out the receivable signals, it is advisable to consult the site http://www.hamqsl.com/solar.html to verify propagation conditions for the various bands.

Conclusions

  • Easy to build antenna, at very low cost, which if positioned properly can give the same satisfaction as a half-wave dipole.
  • Once the antenna was completed with the first QSO I connected to UDINE where the Frecce Tricolori contest was in progress arriving with a signal of R5 S9 practically 450km from Turin at the first connection followed by others at greater distances always depending on propagation. Not bad for a piece of electrical cable.....
