February 2007

This DC-DC power supply is used to boost the 12v battery voltage and stabilize the output compensating for battery voltage drop during discharge. Being able to reach 15V-16V it's suitable for obtaining maximum performance from radio transmission equipment. 

Regulation from 5 to 18Volt satisfies the requirements of many electronic loads.

The addition of a mains circuit with 220V/14V 250VA input transformer, rectifying bridge and filter capacitors, expands the possibilities of use.

 The input voltage can be between 9.0 and 18V and the output voltage is adjustable from 5-18.0V When the input is greater than 12.0V the maximum output current can reach 20A (with fan). The output voltage is adjustable via potentiometer from a minimum value of 5 V to a maximum of 18V.

There is no continuous current adjustment on the output, (pay attention) there is only the tuning of the maximum current passing through the MosFet. 

 Best battery performance is obtained with 12-14V. Below 11.00Volt it still works fine, but maximum current is not achievable.

UC3526

This regulator was chosen because it starts operating at 8V. Symmetrical driver output to control BJT and Mos in Push-Pull configuration.  

Complete voltage and current control functions.

 The working frequency is chosen 30 kHz to avoid large losses with ferrite N27. It is determined by (R5+P3) and C6.

This DC-DC power supply can operate without a fan with an output up to 5A; above 70°C the Klixon thermal switch (to be fastened to the component that heats up the most) engages the fan to cool the power section made up of Mosfets, diodes, transformer, inductors and electrolytic capacitors.

Realization

The realization of the Switching Power Supply involves having knowledge of electronics, equipment and experience in electronic assembly are necessary.

No liability is accepted for damage to things and people and for improper use of the implementation.

 On the left the diode heatsink, in the center the three MosFets in parallel, the cooling fin is sufficient only if with forced ventilation, otherwise mount on a larger heatsink.

The airflow of the 12 V fan should be directed at the components that heat up the most: the fast diodes on the output, filter capacitors and coils, transformer and power MosFet.

In the final version inside a metal cabinet, it is necessary to create an air flow channel that can cool all power components well.

R4 P2 output voltage regulation

The minimum voltage obtainable at the output is equal to the reference voltage present on pin 18 of the 3526 equal to 5.00Volt, in this case the value of P2 is equal to 0 ohm. The maximum voltage is given by the divider formed by P2 on R4. To obtain a complete excursion of P2 from 5 to 18V, it is advisable to mount in place of R4 a 22-turn cermet trimmer of 5 or 10K ohm so as to do a preset of the end-of-travel of P2. With the system running bring the P2 value to end-of-travel and then adjust R4 to have either 16 or 18V full scale, so at minimum we will always have 5V at the output and the full voltage excursion on the linear value of P2. If you want to have an output voltage with excursion from 0V to 18V, you need an external voltage reference to the 3526 with a value of 0.6 lower than the negative.

Transistor Mos-Fet Q1/Q2

 In this case I used RFP50N06 type Power MosFets, three are mounted in parallel for each symmetrical side.

They work fine with all power Mos-Fets with 60V 50A characteristics or higher. They should be mounted on the heatsink using thermal grease to improve heat transfer. Since all three are in parallel I would mount them without insulation towards the heatsink, then use a threaded screw for the common Drain connection directly on the heatsink. The entire heatsink must be fixed with insulating bushings towards the container frame. The two heatsinks must not touch each other.

Power diodes D1

 Diode D1 can be a dual Schottky type rated 60A 50V; unfortunately the working voltage is very close to the breakdown voltage because the spikes on the output for a half-bridge are on the order of 70–90V. Those who can properly design snubbers can attempt to reduce the spikes with a dedicated snubber on each diode. It's clear that using Schottky gives better efficiency and lower temperatures.

Optionally you can adopt 4 Schottky diodes from 60 A 50V in full bridge configuration and put in parallel the two output windings (With the same phase)

In the opposite case fast diodes of 200V 20/30A are mounted (as can be seen in the photo).

Transformer T1

 The ferrite core is a Siemens E55 material type N27. A dedicated bobbin is necessary to facilitate winding.

The primary winding is composed of 2+2 turns made with 12+12 wires of enameled copper rated 0.75mm. The winding must be made last on top of the secondary. The 24 wires must all be wound together to ensure the same length (equal inductance and resistance for each symmetrical winding). You must prepare the 24 segments 60/70cm long before starting to wind.

 The secondary winding consists of 4+4 turns made of 8 wires of 0.75mm enameled copper. The winding must be made first under the primary. The 16 wires must all be wound together to ensure the same length (equal inductance and resistance). You need to prepare 16 segments 100/110 cm long before starting to wind.

Between the windings isolate with 2 turns of Mylar tape or transformer insulating material.

 After finishing winding, the turns must be secured with a few wraps of Mylar or Nomex tape for windings. Optional heat-shrink tubing to protect the output connection.

 The finished transformer assembled with the ferrite should be impregnated under vacuum with transformer varnish, not so much for isolation problems, but to reduce the ultrasonic noise generated during operation.

 The separation of the two primary and secondary windings must be done after removing the enamel for 10-15mm from the end part of the copper wire, then using the tester to identify the start-end of the winding, the 8 + 8 wires are separated to form the two windings.

The resulting terminals must be soldered together with a copper ring terminal.

Inductor L1

 The filter inductor recovered from a 500W ATX power supply is used, the 5V section is used which is sized to support over 30A (ventilated).

 The other windings with smaller diameter wire are not used and must be isolated from each other. The winding made up of three 1.2mm wires must handle all the output current. The three wires must be connected in parallel with each other on the input and output.

8/10 turns 3 x 1.2mm on a 35mm toroidal core.

Inductor L2

In this case too, the second filter inductor from a 500W ATX power supply is used, a few turns of large diameter, wound on ferrite. It must handle all the output current. (max 20A)

8 turns of 2mm wire on a 10mm ferrite core.

Calibration

 Connect a variable power supply to the input and raise the voltage to 12V, we should have the circuit operating; adjust P2 to vary the output voltage. If good range from 5 to 18V is achieved, you can connect a load of at least 5A and retry the output voltage sweep which should reach up to 18V. Voltage shortfalls at the low and high end can depend on the potentiometer value itself and from 

 The P3 trimmer is used to calibrate the frequency to 30 kHz, remove the R1 and R7 on the MOS gate and power only the UC3526, we should have a 50% symmetric square wave, adjust the frequency to 30 kHz (+/-10%)

 Trimmer P1 is used to set the maximum current limiting threshold, it must be adjusted under load to not let the output current exceed 20A when the input is 11.0V.

 Test at maximum load:

For the test at 16V 20A we need a resistor of 16V/20A = 0.80 ohm 350W not easy to find.

If we don't have a variable electronic load (see my other pages), we can create a good load with 12V 55W automobile lamps.

Attention all halogen lamps when cold have a much lower resistance than when lit, therefore at startup they draw up to 5 times the rated current.

This power supply is capable of continuously powering 4 or even 5 lamps in parallel. 

The output voltage must be regulated at no-load to 12.5 Volts. The only precaution is to turn on the lamps one after the other and not all together, this to avoid the strong inrush current that could trigger the battery fuse.

Another trick to use the lamp load is to set the current with the trimmer to the minimum value and then, when the lamps are just lit with a few volts, bring the adjustment to a high value so that the output control takes precedence with voltage regulation.

Read the output voltage at the load and check the voltage drop between load and no-load. Values of 0.5V are acceptable. Larger drops mean the conductors are not of adequate section and/or the input source is not powerful enough and causes internal voltage drop. Be careful that the two sensing wires from the 3526 must be positioned directly on the output terminal strip.

Connection on 12v battery

When it was tested in the laboratory and everything is OK, we can connect the power supply to a 12V battery, it must be a battery with a minimum capacity of 63A/h: 

A 30A fuse is required in series with one of the battery cables. An automotive type fuse works fine and is much more economical.

Battery cables must be 10mmq and their length not exceeding 1.5m.

The output cables must also be 10mmq and max 1.5m.

Input group 220V 300VA

Diagram of the 220V/14V transformer group necessary to operate the 20A Power Supply from the mains.

At least 2000uF per Ampere current draw at output is necessary, this to ensure good voltage regulation. 

I used 4 capacitors of 10000uF 35V in parallel, a 47000uF 35-50V capacitor also works but is harder to find.

The 300VA transformer would ideally be toroidal type with shield between primary and secondary. The electrostatic shield connected to ground is very useful to reduce interference from the mains.

The rectifier bridge must be mounted on a generously sized heatsink.

In case of maximum current draw for long periods, the entire group must have forced ventilation, ideal to take advantage of the same fan as the Switching.

Not essential, but useful a mains filter on the input and possibly transient suppressors between phases and to ground, to reduce the risk of discharges present in the grid.

The connections from the bridge to the capacitors and to the load should be made with 6/10 mm² cable, ideally a 10 mm copper strip 1 mm thick, directly on the terminals of the electrolytic capacitors.

POWER SUPPLY 12V OUTPUT 40+40V for CAR AUDIO AMPLIFIER 100+100Watt rms

May 2007.

Always using the driving base described above, the output stage is easily modifiable to have a higher voltage.

In this case 40+40V with a maximum current draw of 5A is required, the total output power can reach 400W. Therefore you need to beef up the input circuit by putting 5 Mosfets in parallel for each branch.

The transformer primary must also handle higher current so we will put 2+2 turns made of 24+24 enameled copper wires diameter 0.75mm the terminations are joined on a copper lug of appropriate size.

The bridge diodes must be super fast type, it is not possible to use Schottky as they stop at 50V.
The output stage is double and symmetrical, the voltage is adjusted with R19 only on one branch, the other must be of equal voltage value by necessity.
