Is a 3D printer useful in Electronics design?

It can certainly make an excellent contribution to realizing hardware parts in electronics construction. Containers of strange shapes are quickly realizable, for example an enclosure for Arduino, perhaps made in transparent ABS, to see LEDs lit on the board, can easily and quickly be made in less than an hour...

For those who repair and restore equipment and radios, it can be very useful to print the support or bracket in insulating material, or even replicate hard-to-find knobs or covers.

I would say almost essential for model builders, where replicating two identical hand-made components is almost impossible, with the 3D printer you can print infinite pieces all the same from the same drawing. 

Or refine and finalize a component difficult to design, making small modifications after functional print verification, in a few hours you can work the miracle...and file the patent (anyone working in car design will understand me...)

Materials for 3D thermoplastics

Materials filament base for DaVinci are ABS and PLA

  • ABS suitable for more durable applications and even outdoor use, resists UV better, extrusion temperature 210°, heated bed 100°
  • PLA material with better performance, appears more glossy and elastic, also quite robust, but due to its corn-based organic composition, it is not suitable for external applications or humid environments, as it is biodegradable. Extrusion temperature 190°, heated bed at 45°

Here you find a useful guide on materials and other suggestions for 3D printing:  http://nicklievendag.com/filament-guide/

Use

This XYZprinting printer is very easy to use, and is ready to use once removed from the packaging.

Many budget 3D printers are supplied disassembled, but it is not so easy to complete a well-done assembly....!!

  • the heated bed is used to keep the part soft, otherwise material shrinkage during the printing phase could in certain shapes cause visible and significant deformations.
  • The starting point for printing is the glass plate, it is necessary to apply a layer of adhesive glue (UHU glue) to promote adhesion of the first layer, the subsequent layers will fuse together. The glue can be easily cleaned with an alcohol-dampened cloth and the use of a plastic spatula (to avoid scratching the glass).

With PLA material no glue is needed, clean glass is sufficient as a starting base to heat to 40-60°

  • The time to produce a finished piece is very much a function of the size and thus the amount of filament to melt and position. By optimizing the CAD drawing, with experience it's possible to significantly reduce material without compromising structure and robustness.
  • Small objects can also be made solid, but larger structures are better with selected fill parameters to save material without reducing strength. For heights greater than 3mm, values at 15% with hexagonal structure give good rigidity values.

By sectioning a printed piece, in this case 5mm high, it is possible to see the internal structure fill 25%.

3D Printer TRICKS & TIPS

Calibration

- The print bed calibration must be done often, as it is important for a good start of the piece to be printed. It is not a long or difficult procedure; most of the time if it's okay you don't need to intervene. It's important that the contact nozzle be perfectly clean because a slight layer of plastic prevents metal-to-metal contact and the motor continues to push until it breaks or deforms the bed.

Reset cartridges


- The original cartridge contains an eprom that is updated on filament consumption in meters, a new cartridge contains 200m of filament in ABS or PLA, with each print the quantity of material used is subtracted, when the meter reading of the original cartridge is exhausted, the printer stops asking to replace the cartridge. 

All good, but the cost of the original filament cartridge is over 50 euros for 600g of filament; the non-original filament, available on Ebay or Amazon costs 20-25 euros/kg therefore it is certainly worth considering resetting the Eprom to use non-original filament. With the Da Vinci Pro 1.0 Printer it's not a problem since it's possible to use non-original filaments, as well as freely set print parameters.
