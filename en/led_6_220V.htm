Example with 220V 10W power supply, suitable for 3 x LED P4

September 2008

With Power LEDs you don't just make bicycle lamps....

With a little care (towards the 220V) it is not difficult to make an efficient led lamp capable of effectively replacing a 220V Neon tube of 36W.

For testing convenience a heatsink extrusion only 35cm long was used; I recommend developing the project on aluminum sheet 2-3mm thick, 120cm long and 15cm wide with two 30° bends, to better simulate the shape of a neon ceiling fixture and provide good heat dissipation for the LEDs.

The P4 LEDs are fixed to the aluminum heatsink; to use 12V without wasting energy in series regulation, three LEDs must be placed in series. So for good light distribution, two groups of three LEDs are optimal, also for the power consumed, which becomes about 20W.

The LEDs alone have an output field of 120°, which becomes too dispersive to illuminate a work surface, we will therefore use 25° lenses that concentrate the flux well without closing it too much.

The optimal distance between the lamp and the work surface is 1.5 - 1.8m.

This solution with 6 P4 LEDs is also valid for lighting aquariums, LEDs can be used directly without lenses.

 The construction of a 220V LED lamp requires good knowledge of electronics, 

equipment and experience in electronic assembly is required. 

And clear expertise in the use of 220V devices.

No liability is accepted for damage to things and people and for improper use of the implementation.

6 Seoul Zp4 LEDs mounted on a heatsink. In front of the LEDs are positioned 25° lenses.

With this heatsink at operating temperature 35°C is reached, optimal for LED operation.

Electrical circuit diagram

The circuit is simple, as a 220V/12V transformer from 30-50VA is used. Followed by a rectifying diodes bridge filtered by an electrolytic capacitor.

The current regulation necessary for the LEDs is obtained from a series resistor to the group of 3 LEDs.

The recommended resistance value is 1.8 Ohm 3W, but based on the transformer used it is good to measure the current of each single branch to not exceed 0.9 - 1.0 A for each LED branch. If we exceed 1000mA we risk burning out the LEDs easily.

The capacitor can also be avoided, we would however have a pulsating light at 100 Hz, as happens with neon tubes powered by 50 Hz ballast. In this case we would have a lower voltage, it is important to measure the current of each LED branch, perhaps with an index ammeter to have the true current value. (True RMS)

With this configuration we do not carry out current stabilization, but it has been seen that if the 220V is stable, there are no light variation problems.

Realization

The LEDs are fixed on the heatsink with both screws and thermal conductive adhesive.

The lenses are 25° with 35mm diameter for good optical performance. They can be easily fixed with some glue inside a 34mm hole, perhaps on an aluminum sheet that shields the remaining light coming out of the LED.

To illuminate a wider field you can also use 40-45° lenses.

On the rear of the heatsink we will fix the diode bridge, the electrolytic capacitor and the resistors to regulate the current to the LEDs.

Better to use armored resistors in ceramic or aluminum.

The diode bridge is crossed by 2A, tends to heat up, it is better to use a type that can be fixed by screw on the heatsink. 

The toroidal transformer guarantees good isolation between input and output. Use only high-quality components, approved to standards.

Be careful not to use high-frequency electronic transformers, they are smaller and less expensive, suitable only for halogen lamps, this application cannot use them.

Workbench lit with a 36W neon tube without a light fixture. Strong light around and near the tube; on the work surface there's not much light (200 Lux) even though in practice you can work well. In this case you can say that much of the light is scattered throughout the room, requiring much more light to work well than is needed on the work surface alone.

It is a simple example of non-optimization of luminous output with waste of electrical energy.

The same Work bench illuminated with 6 3,6W LEDs. Strong light on the work surface and little dispersion in the room.

In this case we are at 1000Lux on the work surface, a value perhaps excessive for doing common work. 

Therefore, further good energy savings can be achieved by reducing the number of LEDs on to just three for an LED power consumption of 3.7 x3 equal to about 10W. still obtaining about 500Lux on the work plane.

Or distribute the 6 LEDs over a greater length, to illuminate a larger portion of the work surface.

The example is very simple to understand what is the path to carry out optimal lighting and subsequent energy saving in the home environment. 

Comments

With LED lighting excellent results can be obtained both in brightness and energy savings. 

The cost of the whole group can be considered low, (below 50 €) depending on the quality of the material used it can be thought of as use for over 20 years.

A good design can bring 60% energy savings, improving the quality of illumination.

The application is good for domestic use, but can be advantageously applied also in industrial field where the light sources are substantial....
