Sensor cleaning - Cmos or CCD sensor cleaning

photography tips by R.Chirio

One of the most annoying problems encountered using digital reflexes is the presence of dust deposited on the C-mos or CCD sensor.

Even using film dust has always been a problem, but at most it settles on one frame, not on all!

Whoever uses a digital reflex sooner or later (sure!) will have to solve the problem of dust and dirt deposited on the sensor.

It is clear that the sensor is exposed to external agents only when the shutter blades open, but this is enough for tiny dust particles to enter.

The latest reflex camera models feature a high shutter speed 5 ft/s or 8 ft/s .... this generates considerable air circulation inside the chamber, also greatly helped by the mirror movement. It is therefore easy for dirt and dust already easily entered in the mirror area to easily pass on the sensor.

Some rules to follow to extend the time between one and the next sensor cleaning:

● avoid as much as possible exposing the equipment to dust (good quality bags or backpacks and optics and camera body always inside protective bags).

● keep the lenses very clean, especially at the back.

● regularly clean and vacuum dust from photography bags.

● frequently wash with soap and water the plastic caps of lenses and camera body (dust collects in corners). Never leave optics and camera body without caps for long.

● if operating in dusty environments and places, avoid pump zooms (like Canon 100-400 or 35-350 or 28-300) and prefer zooms with internal movements and fixed optics with sealing gaskets.

● when changing the lens keep the reflex pointed downward.

● avoid changing the lens in dusty environments, when it is windy and in crowded environments. (moving around and walking raises a lot of dust).

● it is wrong to walk with the camera hanging from your neck, even if it is uncomfortable, it is better to put it in the bag each time.

● a smoking photographer has a good chance of having optics and sensor with a coating of nicotina....

Verification of dust presence on sensor.

 Frame the clear sky and shoot with the aperture closed at least f.22 or better at f.32 (macro lenses) better to use short focal lengths like 50mm. Better if the focus is set to minimum distance.

It is also fine to photograph a white wall, keep the focus at minimum value, so to have the wall out of focus, always with f.22 or greater.

With Photoshop or other photo editing programs, adjust the levels and contrast to highlight the black spots.
Enlarge the image to actual size and examine it closely, especially at the edges; the presence of dust is visible as tiny sharp dots, while larger, fainter halos indicate dust on the objective lens being used. (It should be cleaned too, before remounting on the DSLR with the sensor cleaned. If dust is present on the internal lenses, it's best to take the lens to a service center.)

HOW TO CLEAN THE SENSOR

WARNING !!  

Sensor cleaning must be done by people experienced in maintenance of the internal parts of cameras, no responsibility is assumed for damage to persons and things, the writer is an expert technician.

Before removing the lens cover, thoroughly clean the entire camera externally, avoid clothing that sheds hair and dust, and importantly try to be in a very enclosed and dust-free environment, without carpets, curtains and absolutely away from smokers and pets, without other people moving around raising dust. It is advisable to dust and wash the floors in the room, and wait a few hours before cleaning the sensor.

A headband on the hair is very useful to prevent hair and dandruff from falling.

You need to get close to an environment similar to an operating room.

If these conditions are not present avoid the sensor cleaning operation and take the camera to the service center.

The opportunity to clean the sensor should also be the opportunity to clean all the lenses, especially on the rear part, the pump zoom like the Canon 100/400 is terrible for accumulating dust inside.

Lens cleaning should be done after each mission to places where dust has been stirred up. The cleaning of the objective lenses should be done with special kits complete with optical fluid, and although this operation is easier than sensor cleaning, it must always be done with maximum care to avoid scratching the optics, using specific equipment.

SENSOR CLEANING

● The reflex must be firmly fixed on a robust tripod.

● Before opening the shutter and uncovering the sensor, clean the camera and the mirror area, otherwise it will take little to bring dust back inside and on the sensor.

● Clean and vacuum dirt from the prism and the entire chamber, also use adhesive tape pointing outward (like Post-it) to catch lint and dust that has settled inside the chamber.
● Activate the sensor cleaning function and open the shutter to bring the sensor into view, a first visual inspection will immediately give us an idea of the severity of dust presence.
● Use magnifying glasses for precision electronic work, because dust is not visible to the naked eye, and also use strong LED lighting, which being cold and high color temperature shows dust better.

● First phase: (easy operation no risk of scratching the sensor)

If you see a few spots or small hairs you can initially try to blow with compressed air. Use the appropriate spray cans with tube, hold the tube 20-30 cm from the sensor, the first blow should not be directed at the sensor as usually impurities and residues come out from the cap and tube, so give a good blow towards the outside. The next one directed at the sensor, keeping the can firmly and vertically held and always 20-30 cm from the CMOS.

At this point it is easy to verify visually if impurities are still present on the sensor, if it looks clean, close and retake the test photo described above.

If the magnified image has no black spots, the problem is solved.

If instead there are still spots and marks on the image you need to do a more thorough cleaning.

● Second phase: (less easy operation, there are risks of scratching the sensor)

The cleaning of the glass surface of the sensor should be done with an electrostatic brush ( Sensor Brush from VisibleDust or SensorSweep (of Copperhill) specific as dimensions for the relative sensor, the Canon 20D needs a 14mm brush. 

Before brushing the sensor, electrostatically charge it by blowing with the compressed air canister on the brush itself for 5/10 seconds (otherwise it won't pick up dust), then make a single pass from left to right. 

Remove the brush and blow away the dust (from the brush) with a strong blast of clean air, and if necessary repeat the operation. It is possible over time to clean the brush with a specific liquid that does not reduce the electrostatic charge.

Close the aperture and retake the test photo and enlarge the image, 100% cleaning like a new camera is not feasible, accepting small dots at the image edge is a good compromise.

● Third phase: (not an easy operation, there are risks of scratching the sensor)

If there are still many annoying spots, you need to do a thorough cleaning of the sensor, this time using the Sensor Clean with the cleaning liquid, there are two types of liquid, the one that removes dust and the one with neutral pH for any non-mineral points of origin like saliva halos or microorganisms...

Put a few drops of liquid  Eclipse on the anti-scratch tissue pad of the Swab and make light passes on the sensor trying to bring the dust to a corner at the bottom, then always with the swab making small rotary movements remove the dirt remaining in the corner. (Do not use the electrostatic brush for this cleaning nor cotton swabs type cotton-fiocc).

With some experience already visual inspection of the sensor allows us to understand if it has become perfectly clean. Close the shutter, carefully check for no dust and lint in the area under and on the mirror and redo the test shot.

I advise not to overdo it with sensor cleaning, do it only if the amount of spots becomes annoying. To give a reference I would say that for amateur use once every six months may be acceptable.

WARNING !! I REPEAT!!

Sensor cleaning must be done by people expert in the maintenance of the internal parts of cameras, no responsibility is assumed for damage to persons and things, it takes very little to scratch the sensor. The writer is an expert technician.
