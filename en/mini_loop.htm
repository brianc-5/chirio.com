Chirio Mini Loop

Active antenna for 20-40-80mt Band

August 2016

Active LOOP antenna, small size, suitable for receiving signals in the HF band. Compared to the MiniWhip, the Loop is a resonant antenna, therefore it requires a matching circuit to best "tune" the antenna to the reception frequency. The bandwidth is typically narrow, a few MHz; therefore this is not to be considered a wideband antenna like the MiniWhip.

This version is made with ABS frame, 3D printed, is small in size with 16cm diameter coil, is meant to be a portable antenna, dimensions and weight are slightly larger than the MiniWhip SR for scanner.

It was chosen to center the operating frequency on the bands most commonly used by Radio amateurs, such as 20, 40 and 80 meters equal to 14, 7 and 3.5 MHz.

It is possible to build a loop for reception of Medium Wave or for VLF up to 10 kHz but the tuning coil must be made with many tens of turns and if a variable capacitor is used (air type) the capacitance value is not easily available commercially. It is necessary to use Varicap diodes.

A direct comparison with the MiniWhip immediately shows that the Loop is quieter, and in certain cases it can even be used indoors.

Electrical circuit diagram

Circuit diagram active antenna MiniLoop variable capacitor version.

  • The resonant circuit is composed of the Loop coil and the variable capacitor, the capacitor is used to vary the resonance frequency.
  • The tunable capacitor of 240-280pF can be recovered from an old AM radio. The minimum value should reach 10-15pF, otherwise the tuning is not achieved above 14 MHz. Possibly to go up in frequency only 3 turns are wound, or to go down below 3.5 MHz you go to 5 turns. See table.

- Resistor R1 (trimmer) serves to attenuate the incoming signal, also called "Q killer"; it must be adjusted to achieve the best amplitude without reaching saturation that can trigger oscillations on the FET; the correct value can range from 10K to 100K ohm. For those without space constraints, a potentiometer can also be used to adjust tuning sensitivity to maximum each time.

  • The FET amplifier having high impedance does not condition the input tuning, the second stage with 2N2222A amplifies the signal by 10-15dB.
  • With 9V batteries the current draw is 5-8mA therefore an alkaline battery lasts several hours.
  • The tunable bandwidth is very much a function of the values used and parasitic capacitances in play; it is not guaranteed that all three bands can be received and tuned. Locally review the winding, or add capacitors in parallel or in series with the CV to get back in band.

Characteristics

 

  • With 9V battery the current draw is approximately 5mA. 
  • Bandwidth from 3.5 MHz to 14.5 MHz (4 turns diam. 16cm)
  • Bandwidth at the resonance point 500 kHz
  • Output impedance is 200 ohm suitable for pretty much all receiver inputs.

Coil construction

- The tested antenna is made by winding 4 turns of enameled copper wire diam. 0.5mm on a 16cm diameter support, this way we get a "tuning" from 3.5 to 14.5 MHz while for other tunings still with 16cm diameter, see the following table.

Tips for using the MiniLoop:

  • Antenna suitable for external portable use, it also works well to mount it vertically on a receiver of larger size, perhaps with a rigid extension for antenna connectors.
  • The MiniLoop has modest directional characteristics; by rotating on its vertical axis the antenna can better center the signal and verify the reception angle. Not having a reflector like Yagi antennas, it's not possible to know the source but only the emission angle.
  • Via knob you perform the tuning, to have the maximum signal, every time you change tuning on the radio you need to search for the best signal.
  • On request you can insert a small switch to have high range and low range, so to cover with a single antenna the ranges, 3.6 - 7 and 14 MHz

- In this last period of 2015 there are often poor propagation conditions, due to "Solar Spots" that could cancel the signals receivable from the MiniWhip, it is recommended to consult the site http://www.hamqsl.com/solar.html to verify propagation conditions for the various bands.
