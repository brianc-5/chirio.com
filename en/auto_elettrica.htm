Considerations Electric car (battery powered)

What to do, buy, rent or wait? by R.Chirio

Batteries & energyRoberto Chirio

Considerations

April 2022 /January 2025

Premise, I have worked many years in the Fiat group, both in electronics design for automotive devices and in the field as a workshop inspector, I therefore have good knowledge of components and techniques for thermal and electric motors. I can therefore allow myself some considerations and ideas about the electric car....

Types of Hybrid cars (dual motor, internal combustion engine + electric motor with batteries): 

  • - Mild Hybrid  or lightweight hybrid like the Panda or Suzuki models
  • - Full Hybrid  namely Hybrid of greater complexity and range see the various Toyota
  • - Plug-in Hybrid  or Hybrid with the possibility of recharging at a power outlet, see Audi, BMW, Volkswagen

What are they for? Fundamentally to enter and exit Cities, where there are restrictions on combustion engine circulation, in practice they are cars, expensive, with complex electronics, unreliable and high consumption (while you drive with heat engine, it also charges the batteries), they lose value when you sell them, and it costs a lot to replace the batteries ....they are the transition to the electric car....DO NOT BUY THEM, rent them for 6 months and then decide if you want to keep it or replace it!!!!

- Fully Electric

It is the car that is designed to be electric, that is it is not an adaptation or transformation of existing vehicles. It is born with a precise idea of how to arrange the engine, batteries and control electronics.

THE SMALLEST (6000€)

 

The Full Electric AMI with 6KW motor and range of 70Km. (Maybe a little too small!!!)

THE MOST FAMOUS (54000€)

The Full Electric TESLA model 3 with 360KW motor and 550Km runtime. (Maybe a bit too large and expensive)

What do the two electric cars above have in common? .....

How long does the frame last....20 years at least, how long do electric motors last 20 years at least, how long do inverters and onboard electronic controls last 10-20years.............how long do batteries last?...maybe 5 years!!!

The Fixed installed battery is expensive and difficult to replace (in the case of Tesla it is worth 30% of the cost of a new car), having the battery installed inside the car, is a serious design mistake..... the battery is a consumable component and must be replaceable easily and quickly, just like changing tires and brake pads. It's a wrong concept to stop for recharging; it takes a long time for an average recharge, and fast recharges damage batteries.

Electric cars will succeed when standard packs containing batteries are used; the standard pack must have the container and connections identical among all cars, must have a rail for easy and quick insertion (somewhat like what happens with some e-bikes) and such packs, numbering 4–8, will supply power to the car. (Prestige cars, more slots available for higher power)

Cars will have to be sold without a battery, while batteries will be the element to rent, rented from those who now sell gasoline, for example AGIP-ENI, Api-IP, Q8 or other distributors. They are the ones who, in a few years, once fuel stocks are exhausted, will have to supply charged batteries to be installed on the fly in the appropriate slots under the seats, in short additional elements like fuel..........

Rechargeable batteries are still in continuous improvement and will be for many years, we can say that every 2-3 years the capacity doubles with the same weight, therefore the type of individual battery cell will be continuously updated, to the advantage of the consumer, these cells will always be mounted inside the standard battery pack which over time will increase the accumulated power, but will, precisely, always be compatible with the slots of older vehicles, a vehicle from 10 years ago with the new packs can only benefit from runtime and performance advantages...

The electric car, sold without batteries can take a very competitive price, considering precisely that it has a lifespan of 20 years, with very little maintenance, only the replacement costs for tire wear, brake pads, windshield wiper blades and little else..... while the batteries will be guaranteed by whoever rents them... they are not a problem for the driver, they are changed every time you go to the distributor not for fuel anymore but for batteries. Eventually when the cycle is well underway, home charging won't be needed anymore.

Continue....

After various emails exchanged with whoever wrote to me, I can say that by the end of 2024, vehicles of all types with electric motors have no great market, the public rejected hybrid vehicles and even more so pure electric ones. Purchase costs are too high as is maintenance. Hybrid vehicles in addition to the high purchase price also have unjustified, unsustainable maintenance and service costs for anyone who has already spent themselves on 25-30000€ for the new purchase, ends up paying 500 to 1700€ per year for necessary service to stay within the 5-8 year warranty. Unacceptable prices for a normal family..........much better to stick with a thermal engine vehicle, better if Diesel...........it costs much less to do a normal repair and change consumables like oil, filters, tires at a small workshop, better if family-run where it's possible to talk with the mechanic. I would avoid as much as possible the big branches and dealerships that have too many costs they pass on to customers. Even better for those who know how, changing oil and filters yourself you spend very little buying online. The highest cost is tire replacement which unfortunately must be done at the tire shop because of balancing. You can however buy tires online at low costs.

I would recommend the pure full electric vehicle only to those who have the possibility of home charging with a photovoltaic solar system, it is too expensive to charge from the columns. The incentives should first of all be given to those who demonstrate solar charging, so as not to overload the national electrical network.

Chinese cars are much cheaper with low purchase and maintenance costs. New types of batteries are being developed and someone is thinking of using removable batteries.....
